## Thread

**1/8**

Crypto apps can see wallets. But wallets are not users.

One person may use several wallets. One wallet may interact with many protocols. Raw addresses show activity—not the human journey behind it.

That is the gap @SegmentoAI is building to close. 🧵

**2/8**

Most teams can answer “what happened on-chain?”

Far fewer can answer:

• Who brought this user?
• What did they do before arriving?
• Are they profitable or just farming?
• Where else do they deploy capital?
• What are they likely to do next?

**3/8**

Think of Segmento as a user-intelligence layer for Solana.

It connects on-chain behavior with off-chain acquisition context, turning fragmented wallet activity into a clearer picture of users, cohorts, journeys, and intent.

**4/8**

Why does the acquisition source matter?

Imagine two wallets deposit the same amount.

One arrived through a trusted creator and stays for months. The other came from a short-term incentive and leaves as soon as rewards stop.

Same deposit. Very different user quality.

**5/8**

A trading app could use this intelligence to learn which channels attract active, profitable traders—not simply the most wallet connections.

That means better creator partnerships, smarter campaigns, and less budget spent on activity that never becomes retention.

**6/8**

A DeFi protocol could identify cohorts that:

• bridge capital from specific ecosystems
• use complementary protocols
• return after their first interaction
• grow balances over time
• respond to education rather than incentives

Raw transactions become actionable segments.

**7/8**

The important shift is from wallet analytics to user intelligence.

Instead of treating every address as an isolated identity, teams can analyze behavior patterns, acquisition paths, capital movement, and predicted intent—while making product and growth decisions from evidence.

**8/8**

On-chain data tells you what happened.

User intelligence helps explain who drove it, where they came from, why it matters, and what may happen next.
